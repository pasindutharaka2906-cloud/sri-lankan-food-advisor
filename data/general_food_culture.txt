Sri Lankan food culture is deeply rooted in the island's history, combining indigenous traditions with influences from Indian, Arab, Malay, and European traders and colonizers.

The staple meal is rice and curry, which consists of a large portion of rice accompanied by a variety of side dishes. These side dishes typically include a meat or fish curry, several vegetable curries, a mallum (shredded green leafy vegetables with coconut), and a sambol.

Hoppers (Aappa) are a popular breakfast and dinner item. They are bowl-shaped pancakes made from a fermented batter of rice flour and coconut milk. They can be served plain or with an egg cooked in the center, and are typically eaten with spicy lunumiris or curries.

String Hoppers (Idiyappam) are steamed rice noodles pressed into circular mats. They are commonly served with a thin coconut milk gravy (Kiri Hodi) and a spicy sambol.

Dietary considerations: Many Sri Lankan vegetable curries are naturally vegan as they use coconut milk instead of dairy. However, 'Maldive fish' (cured tuna) is frequently used as a flavor enhancer in many dishes, including sambols and vegetable curries. Those seeking vegetarian or vegan options should always ask if Maldive fish has been added.
